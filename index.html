BEFORE YOU BOARD THE PLANE

Know Japan Before You Go

Japan is amazing, but your first trip becomes much easier when you know the little things beforehand.

🚆

Trains Are Serious Business

Japanese trains are famous for being clean, efficient, and extremely punctual.

💴

Bring Some Cash

Japan is highly modern, but cash is still useful, especially at small restaurants and local shops.

👟

You Will Walk. A Lot.

Japanese stations and neighborhoods can involve a surprising amount of walking.

🗑️

Where Are The Trash Cans?

Public trash cans can be surprisingly difficult to find.

🤫

Quiet On The Train

Public transportation tends to be calm and quiet.

♨️

Onsen Has Rules

Japanese hot springs have etiquette that first-time visitors should know.

SMALL THINGS THAT HELP A LOT

First-Timer Survival Tips

Click any card to see what you should prepare, what to bring, and what you should expect when you're actually in Japan.

01
💳

Get an IC Card

Make trains, buses, vending machines, and convenience-store purchases easier.

02
🗺️

Use Google Maps

Learn how to read train lines, platforms, transfers, exits, and walking routes.

03
🏪

Know Convenience Stores

7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson can save you when you need food, drinks, cash, or basic supplies.

04
🧳

Don't Overpack

Leave room for souvenirs, snacks, clothes, anime merchandise, and the things you absolutely did not plan to buy.

05
🔌

Prepare Your Electronics

Bring the right charger, power bank, cables, and travel adapter so your phone survives the day.

06
📱

Get Mobile Internet

Internet access makes navigation, translation, bookings, and emergencies much easier.

07
👟

Wear Comfortable Shoes

Your feet are going to work overtime. Comfortable shoes can completely change your trip.

08
🗑️

Carry a Small Trash Bag

Public trash cans aren't always easy to find, so carrying a small bag is surprisingly useful.
